## Campaign review

**The campaign's highest-ranked cobalt-free computational prediction was Li8TiMn11(NiO8)4, with an energy density of 701 Wh/kg at 4.75 V.**

### What the campaign taught

- Round 1 showed that nickel substitution in LiMn2O4 gave Li3Mn5NiO12 at 646 Wh/kg and 4.39 V, while dilute titanium substitution in Li2Mn3NiO8 gave Li8TiMn11(NiO8)4 at 701 Wh/kg and 4.75 V.
- Round 2 tested whether other dopants could improve on titanium, but Li8Mn11V(NiO8)4 reached 690 Wh/kg and Li6Mn11AlO24 reached only 488 Wh/kg, so neither chemical idea outperformed titanium on the same spinel family.
- Round 3 confirmed the ranking on Li2Mn3NiO8, with titanium at 701 Wh/kg ahead of vanadium at 690 Wh/kg and zirconium in Li8ZrMn11(NiO8)4 at 676 Wh/kg, and titanium also had the lowest energy above the hull per substitution among those three.
- Olivine proposals changed after repeated novelty failures: manganese and nickel substitutions in LiFePO4 were already known, whereas silicon-for-phosphorus substitution in LiMnPO4 produced Li4Mn4SiP3O16 at 674 Wh/kg, 3.92 V, and 172 mAh/g.
- On LiMn2O4, nickel retained energy density better than titanium or aluminium, ranking 646 Wh/kg for Li3Mn5NiO12 ahead of 574 Wh/kg for Li6TiMn11O24 and 488 Wh/kg for Li6Mn11AlO24.

### What worked

- Li8TiMn11(NiO8)4 passed every filter with voltage of 4.75 V, capacity of 147 mAh/g, energy density of 701 Wh/kg, volume change of 3.4%, and energy above the hull of 0.003 eV/atom.
- Li8Mn11V(NiO8)4 and Li8ZrMn11(NiO8)4 also passed and ranked as finalists, with energy densities of 690 Wh/kg and 676 Wh/kg, voltages of 4.69 V and 4.72 V, and volume changes of 3.4% and 3.9%, respectively.
- These spinels worked because dilute substitution preserved high voltage and capacity near 143 to 147 mAh/g while keeping volume change below 4% and energy above the hull at or below 0.016 eV/atom.

### What failed

- Li4MnFe3(PO4)4 and Li4Fe3Ni(PO4)4 failed the novelty test because their compositions were already in the database, and a possible cause is the hypothesis that these olivine ratios overlap heavily explored chemistry.
- Li6Mn11AlO24 passed the filters but was not a finalist, with energy density of only 488 Wh/kg, voltage of 3.85 V, and capacity of 127 mAh/g; a possible cause is the hypothesis that aluminium pillaring diluted redox-active capacity more than it helped stability.
- Li6TiMn11O24 also passed but reached only 574 Wh/kg on LiMn2O4, compared with 701 Wh/kg for titanium on Li2Mn3NiO8; a possible cause is the hypothesis that titanium benefits the nickel-containing high-voltage spinel more than the plain manganese spinel.

### Next campaign

> Find cobalt-free lithium-ion cathodes with higher predicted energy density than Li8TiMn11(NiO8)4 while keeping low volume change and low energy above the hull.

Starting from: Li2Mn3NiO8, LiMnPO4, LiMn2O4.

- Vary the dilute titanium-for-manganese count in Li2Mn3NiO8 around the Li8TiMn11(NiO8)4 composition and test each arrangement for voltage, capacity, volume change, and energy above the hull.
- Try titanium plus nickel co-substitution in LiMn2O4 to combine the 646 Wh/kg nickel result with the stabilising titanium idea from the champion spinel.
- Extend silicon-for-phosphorus substitution in LiMnPO4 beyond Li4Mn4SiP3O16 with nearby silicon counts, checking whether capacity near 172 mAh/g can be retained with lower volume change than 7.2%.
- Avoid repeating the exact manganese-for-iron and nickel-for-iron olivine ratios that failed novelty, and avoid aluminium-only or zirconium-only spinel routes that ranked below titanium.

The champion Li8TiMn11(NiO8)4 combined the highest energy density at 701 Wh/kg with volume change of 3.4% and energy above the hull of 0.003 eV/atom, so nearby titanium and nickel chemistry is the most promising direction. The silicon-doped olivine Li4Mn4SiP3O16 was the only novel olivine route to pass, at 674 Wh/kg and 172 mAh/g, but its 7.2% volume change leaves room for improvement.
