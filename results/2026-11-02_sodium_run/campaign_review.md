## Campaign review

**The campaign's champion computational prediction is Na3PSeS3, which passed stability, band gap and sodium-metal passivation tests but only tied its parent in diffusion at 600 K.**

### What the campaign taught

- After Na5PWS8 failed sodium-metal contact, later rounds avoided reducible tungsten and shifted toward isovalent selenium, antimony and germanium substitutions, whereas halogen-plus-vacancy and antimony-selenium proposals still failed their fixed tests.
- Selenium for sulfur in Na3PS4 held across doses: Na3PSeS3 had an energy above the hull of 0.002 eV/atom and a band gap of 2.27 eV, while Na6P2SeS7 had an energy above the hull of 0.002 eV/atom and a band gap of 2.26 eV.
- The same selenium idea did not hold on the antimony sulfide parent, as Na3SbSeS3 had a band gap of 1.77 eV and was rejected.
- Germanium for silicon in the NASICON parent also held from Na3Zr2SiGePO12, with an energy above the hull of 0.002 eV/atom and a band gap of 4.20 eV, to fully substituted Na3Zr2Ge2PO12, with an energy above the hull of 0.003 eV/atom and a band gap of 3.98 eV.
- Antimony for phosphorus in Na6SbPS8 passed with a band gap of 2.24 eV and passivating behaviour, but its energy above the hull of 0.008 eV/atom was higher than the selenium-for-sulfur candidates.

### What worked

- Na3PSeS3 ranked first among the finalists because its energy above the hull per substitution was 0.018 eV, below 0.026 eV for Na3Zr2Ge2PO12 and 0.031 eV for Na6P2SeS7, and it was passivating against sodium metal with a band gap of 2.27 eV.
- Na3PSeS3 was the only finalist with simulated diffusion, giving a diffusion coefficient of 2.16e-07 cm²/s at 600 K and diffusion against the parent of 0.94 ± 0.71, which the fixed rules recorded as tied.
- Na3Zr2Ge2PO12 and Na6P2SeS7 passed as finalists with passivating behaviour and band gaps of 3.98 eV and 2.26 eV respectively, although diffusion was not simulated for either compound.

### What failed

- Na5PWS8 failed contact with sodium metal as non-passivating with a reaction energy of 1.452 eV/atom, possibly because reducible tungsten may have formed metallic tungsten.
- Na3SbSeS3 was rejected for a band gap of 1.77 eV, below the 1.8 eV threshold, possibly because the antimony-selenium combination may have lowered the band gap more than phosphorus-selenium.
- Na5P2S7Br was rejected for an energy above the hull of 0.061 eV/atom, above the 0.05 eV/atom threshold, possibly because bromine substitution combined with a sodium vacancy may have destabilised the sulfide framework.
- Na3HfZrSi2PO12 was rejected because its composition was already known, possibly because hafnium-for-zirconium expansion may have reproduced an existing NASICON composition rather than a distinct prediction.

### Next campaign

> Find sodium solid electrolyte computational predictions from Na3PS4, Na3SbS4 or Na3Zr2Si2PO12 that improve diffusion against their parent while staying passivating against sodium metal.

Starting from: Na3PS4, Na3SbS4, Na3Zr2Si2PO12.

- Test minimal co-doping in Na3PS4 with one to two selenium substitutions plus one antimony substitution, without sodium vacancies.
- Test single-site tellurium for sulfur in Na3PS4 without sodium vacancies, checking energy above the hull and band gap against the fixed thresholds.
- Run diffusion simulations at 600 K for the already passing Na6P2SeS7, Na3Zr2Ge2PO12 and Na6SbPS8 before proposing further analogues.

Selenium and germanium expansions passed with energies above the hull near 0.002 eV/atom and band gaps above 2.26 eV, but the only simulated diffusion result was tied with its parent. The next campaign should therefore test whether small co-doping changes diffusion while avoiding the tungsten, bromine-vacancy and antimony-selenium failures seen here.
